        What should a useful review record contain?

        A review record should make the scope, evidence and reasoning understandable to someone who was not involved in the original assessment. Record the document revision and the question being addressed before describing the conclusion.

        • Identify the source requirements and the information reviewed.
        • Explain the basis of the decision, including assumptions and limitations.
        • State any outstanding action, its owner and what will demonstrate that it is complete.
        How should you handle incomplete or conflicting evidence?

        Separate what the submission demonstrates from what remains uncertain. A missing drawing, inconsistent calculation or unsupported statement should become a specific clarification request, rather than an unstated assumption.

        Describe the gap, explain why it affects the review and identify the information needed to resolve it. Keep the original evidence and the response together so a later reviewer can follow what changed.

        When is a follow-up action ready to close?

        Close an action when the requested evidence has been received and checked against the question it was intended to resolve. Receiving a revised document does not, by itself, demonstrate that the issue has been addressed.

        Record the document revision, the check performed and the resulting decision. If the response raises another question, state that question explicitly and assign the next action.
